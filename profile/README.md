## 🌐 About NeuralStack | MS

**NeuralStack | MS** is an AI Security Engineering ecosystem dedicated to understanding, designing, testing, and securing modern AI systems — from large language model (LLM) applications to autonomous, tool-using agents.

The mission is to build a comprehensive, practical AI security knowledge ecosystem combining **research, system architecture, engineering practices, open-source implementations, and education**. Aim to help engineers and organizations build AI systems whose behavior, authority, and operational risks can be understood, evaluated, and controlled.

Approach AI security as a **systems engineering discipline**, not merely a collection of prompt-level defenses. Secure AI requires explicit trust boundaries, enforceable authorization, constrained execution, measurable assurance, and continuous evaluation.

## 🔐 Core Focus Areas

| Domain | Engineering questions |
| --- | --- |
| **LLM application security** | How do we defend against prompt injection, sensitive-data exposure, unsafe tool use, and insecure integrations? |
| **AI agent security** | How do we constrain delegated authority, tool permissions, memory, and multi-agent interactions? |
| **Trust boundaries & authorization** | How do we separate model decisions from policy enforcement and execution? |
| **AI red teaming & evaluation** | How do we design reproducible adversarial tests and measure security control effectiveness? |
| **Secure AI architecture** | How do we build defense-in-depth across models, retrieval, APIs, data stores, and infrastructure? |
| **Security research & intelligence** | How do we turn emerging vulnerabilities, standards, and research findings into actionable engineering guidance? |

## 🧪 Research & Engineering

The work explores security questions through threat modeling, reference architectures, experimental prototypes, adversarial evaluation, and documented engineering trade-offs.

### Research directions

- **Agent authority boundaries:** separation of agent planning, authorization verification, and privileged execution.
- **Sandboxing and delegated permissions:** complementary controls for limiting what AI agents can do.
- **LLM application red teaming:** systematic testing of prompt injection, trust-boundary failures, and unsafe tool interactions.
- **Secure-by-design agent workflows:** least privilege, policy enforcement, auditability, and failure containment.
- **Evaluation and assurance:** evidence-based assessment of security properties and control limitations.

**Research principle:** A proof of concept, simulation, or local test is not a general security guarantee. Distinguish hypotheses, observed results, reproduced findings, and independently validated claims.

## 🧭 Engineering Approach

```text
Threat modeling
      ↓
Trust-boundary and authority analysis
      ↓
Security architecture and control design
      ↓
Implementation and adversarial testing
      ↓
Evaluation, documentation, and iteration
```

Favor **least privilege**, **defense in depth**, **explicit policy enforcement**, **secure defaults**, **reproducible testing**, and **transparent limitations**.

## 📚 Intelligence & Advisory

**NeuralStack | MS Intelligence & Advisory** is a initiative for connecting AI security research with engineering decisions. Its intended areas of work include:

- **Security intelligence:** tracking relevant threats, vulnerabilities, standards, and technical developments.
- **Applied research:** investigating concrete AI security questions and engineering controls.
- **Technical advisory:** helping teams reason about architecture, risk, and security requirements.
- **Education:** translating research into accessible, actionable engineering resources.

Any future assessment or advisory offering will define its scope and evidence requirements explicitly. Technical security assessments should not be confused with penetration testing or formal compliance certification.

## 🤝 Collaboration & Responsible Research

Welcome technically grounded discussion, constructive criticism, reproducible counterexamples, and collaboration on secure AI engineering.

When contributing or reporting findings:

1. State the affected system, trust boundary, and security claim precisely.
2. Provide reproducible steps or evidence when disclosure is appropriate.
3. Separate demonstrated behavior from assumptions and broader conclusions.
4. Avoid publishing secrets, private data, or exploit details that could harm third parties.
5. Follow each repository's contribution and security-reporting guidance where available.

For sensitive vulnerabilities, **do not post exploit details in public issues**. Use a repository's private vulnerability-reporting channel if enabled.
